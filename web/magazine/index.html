Gurgle

Playboy was paper.

The scene, the clubs and the argument all grew off an object people kept in their houses. Gurgle is the magazine, and it has never been a thing you can hold. It does not exist yet.

What it is made of. What members wrote — answers to the three questions from the feed, chosen by an editor and printed under the number of the person who wrote them. No names, because there are none. Nothing is commissioned and nothing is written by staff.

Except the first one. A first edition made of member writing needs members, and there are none yet. So for the first one it can come from anybody: send something below without an account and without a name. After that the magazine is made the way it is meant to be made.

The three questions

  • 01Do I Have What it Takes To Run This Country?
  • 02Why Do I Want To Run Away?
  • 03for better taste…

The cover is scannable. It is registered the same way any advertisement is, so pointing a camera at the front of the magazine opens the platform — the scanner does not care whether it is looking at a poster, a sticker or a cover. The magazine advertises the thing that made it.

Why an object at all. A website is read where it is found and nowhere else. A magazine goes home with somebody, sits on a table, gets picked up by a person who was not looking for it, and travels to places the run club does not. It is the only part of this that can leave the city on its own.

How you get one. Cash, in person, at the run — the same as the sticker and the donation, and for the same reason. Money you can leave with.

It does not exist yet. Nothing has been decided — not the size, not the price, not how often, not how it is printed. What it needs first is enough writing to make one out of, and that comes from people turning up.

Send something

No account, no name, no address. One person reads everything that arrives; some of it goes in the first edition. Answer one of the three, the same as a member would.
