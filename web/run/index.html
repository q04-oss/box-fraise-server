The route. From the park, along Whyte Avenue, out to the Canadian Pacific railyard, and back. It is a loop with a destination, and the destination is the point.

What it is arguing for. That Whyte Avenue should be closed to cars. It is the densest, most walked street in Edmonton and it is still organised around moving vehicles through it. The run is a weekly, physical version of that argument — a street being used the way we say it should be used, by people, at the hour when it is quiet enough to imagine.

Why the railyard. Not the rail line — the yard. The tracks that used to cross Whyte are gone, and people reach for that as though it settled the question. It did not. The yard is still there, and the yard is the reason a car-free Whyte Avenue has never happened.

So the run goes and looks at it. Advocacy usually points at what it wants. This points at what is in the way.

Just turn up. There is nothing to sign and nobody to email first. Saturday, 8am, the park.

What it is part of. Box Fraise is the next generation of Playboy magazine, and an attempt to imagine what the next generation of business school is going to be. The run is the first thing it does — a community organised around what is good for the nervous system, on the theory that better people make better work. The whole argument is here.
