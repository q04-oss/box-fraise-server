Whyte Avenue run club

Whyte Avenue should be pedestrian only.

Every Saturday at 8, at Dr. Wilbert McIntyre Park. You do not need to be fast and you do not need to know anyone.

What this one is for. Whyte Avenue pedestrian only, and the ultimate entertainment destination. Which takes:

  • 01A land trust, started by the City of Edmonton, that every Edmontonian is bought into.
  • 02The Princess Theatre as the most prestigious private theatre school in the world.
  • 03Local business championed through affordable rent.
  • 04Every business part of a case study project, which Edmontonians take part in by showing up in person.
  • 05The Edmonton Protocol: how to start a business in a new, futuristic way.

Then make the protocol impressive enough that entrepreneurs from across the country want to join it and be incubated through the land trust. Part of it is helping a business open locations across the country, which is the ultimate presence of Edmonton in it.

Where the protocol comes from. Box Fraise is an attempt at the next generation of business school, and the point of a business school is the community it builds. The case studies are the class, run in person with the businesses on the street. The protocol is the by-product: what gets learned that way, written down. The argument for the school.

Why Whyte. Whyte Avenue is the densest, most walked street in Edmonton and it is still organised around moving vehicles through it. The run is a weekly, physical version of that argument — a street being used the way we say it should be used, by people, at the hour when it is quiet enough to imagine.

What a Box Fraise run club is. It builds the community the platform needs. Turning up is the count: this many people want businesses to adopt it.

What every run club advocates for. Businesses leaving tipping out of their business model. The ones we celebrate for it.

The route. From the park, along Whyte Avenue, out to the Canadian Pacific railyard, and back. It is a loop with a destination, and the destination is the point.

Why the railyard. Not the rail line — the yard. The tracks that used to cross Whyte are gone, and people reach for that as though it settled the question. It did not. The yard is still there, and the yard is the reason a car-free Whyte Avenue has never happened.

So the run goes and looks at it. Advocacy usually points at what it wants. This points at what is in the way.

Topless, if you want to be. Anyone can run without a shirt — men or women, one rule for everybody, which is the thing the law does not manage. The City of Edmonton already allows it at its own pools, for every patron, whatever their gender. The run asks why that stops at the pool doors. Nobody is expected to, and nobody is asked why not.

No photographs. Not of anyone, at any point. Turning up is not permission to be photographed, and this is the run club of a project whose whole argument is that you get asked first.

Just turn up. There is nothing to sign and nobody to email first. Saturday, 8am, the park.

What it is part of. Box Fraise is the next generation of Playboy magazine, and an attempt to imagine what the next generation of business school is going to be. The run is the first thing it does — a community organised around what is good for the nervous system, on the theory that better people make better work. The whole argument is here.
