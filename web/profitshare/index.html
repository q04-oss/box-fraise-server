Profit share

A cut of what your attention earned.

The people an advertising machine is sold on should be on the revenue side of the sale. None of it exists yet — the argument does, and so does the arithmetic.

Playboy paid its readers in a magazine. That is the arrangement every ad-funded business runs on, not an abuse of it: the attention of the people using the thing is the inventory, it is sold, and none of the money comes back to them. Box Fraise is the next generation of that machine, so it inherits the arrangement unless something is done about it.

Consent alone does not do it. A checkbox costs a platform nothing and changes nothing about who the machine is for. Consent without a share is permission. Consent with a share is a wage.

Every fight about this has gone the other direction. The gig economy's actual innovation was classifying labour downward — Uber spent years and a fortune arguing its drivers were not workers, lost in the UK Supreme Court in 2021, and then bought the loss back in California with a ballot initiative that traded employment for a floor. Going the other way is only affordable at the beginning, at nothing, before there is anyone whose permission is needed.

So the share is currently zero, because the profit is. That is worth writing down rather than leaving to be discovered. A share of nothing is nothing, and it stays nothing until this project sells something.

The run club is not a job

Nobody is paid to turn up at six in the morning, and nobody will be. That is what makes turning up mean anything — it is evidence precisely because there is no money in it, and nothing happens if you miss cannot survive a wage. Attach a rate to running and the rate becomes the reason, and the people it attracts leave for a better one.

Employment attaches only to work that is unmistakably work.

  • 01Carrying a mark. A run across a province with an advertiser's name on it. A sandwich board is a job and always has been.
  • 02Writing an edition. The magazine is commissioned work the moment anybody is paid for it.
  • 03Running a leg. A fixed distance on a fixed day, filed at the end of it.

The first one that could be a job

Somebody running across the country, paid out of the advertising. It is a defined term with a start and an end, which is a shape employment law already handles. St. John's to Victoria is roughly seven thousand two hundred kilometres; at forty-two a day that is about a hundred and seventy of them.

Distance~7,200 km
At 42 km a day~170 days
Eight hours a day, Alberta minimum~$20,400
Overtime, vacation pay, CPP, EI, WCBthe rest
Wages for one crossing~$30,000

An estimate, before a support vehicle, food or a bed. It is what an advertiser would be asked to cover.

Which is a legible thing to sell. Not reach on a website, which is why there is no page here selling that — a route, a number of days, and a person. One serious sponsor rather than a hundred small ones.

And employment is the point rather than the paperwork. Somebody on the shoulder of the Trans-Canada for six months gets hit eventually. An employee is covered by workers' compensation with wage replacement; a contractor with a sponsorship is a person with an injury and no income, and the project ends the same afternoon.

The timesheet and the autobiography are the same rows

Employment needs a record of hours. A share needs a record of participation. The calendar needs a record of where you were. Those are one file: every run, every leg, every day on the road, written down once. What you are owed is decided by the same document somebody would want to read.

And it points at the month everyone takes at once. A platform whose members are users can only stop posting. A platform whose members are workers with a share can strike, including against this one. That is a demonstration rather than an essay, and it is the version worth building.

What has not been decided

  • 01The form. Employment brings minimum wage, payroll and the obligations that go with it. A co-operative — a real filing in Alberta — distributes surplus to members as a patronage return based on how much they took part, and owes no wages at all. The second is closer to what this is.
  • 02The share. What fraction, of which revenue. A percentage published on a page is a different promise from “a share”, and only the published one is worth making.
  • 03Who is in it. Everyone with a number, or only the people who took part that period. Whether a member with two hundred runs outranks a founder.

It would be paid in cash, at a run. That is already the only payment mechanism this project has designed — across a counter, in person — and the only one that reaches somebody without a bank account.
