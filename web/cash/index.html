Cash

Money you can leave with.

Across a counter, at a clubhouse, from a hand. It does not exist yet — the argument for it does.

This is not a preference about payment. Every card transaction has a third party in it who takes a cut, keeps a record, and can say no. The cut is the part people notice and the least important of the three. The record is worse. The veto is the one that decides what a platform is allowed to become — a private company, answerable to nobody here, holding an opinion about what you may sell.

Nobody is ever asked. What you buy becomes advertising aimed back at you, assembled by companies you have no relationship with, and you did not agree to it and cannot see it. It is the largest example in ordinary life of the thing this whole project exists to argue against. Box Fraise is a consensual advertising engine; it cannot run on money that asks nobody.

And cash is what some people have. Being unbanked is common among people without housing, and a card-only world is a door they cannot open. The clubhouse was built for its own reasons. Money that works from a hand is what lets somebody living in it take part in everything else.

Running away needs three things

  • 01A roof to leave from and come back to. The clubhouse.
  • 02Time you are allowed to take. The month everyone takes at once.
  • 03Money you can carry. This.

You cannot withdraw from a system that holds your money. Wages arrive in an account, at a bank; a network approves each purchase and keeps the record. That is not money you can leave with, it is money you can be traced by and cut off from. The plebeians could walk out of Rome because what they had was in their hands.

Across a counter, and nowhere else. You cannot join this platform remotely, post remotely, be marked present remotely, or put money in remotely. Money would work the same way — it enters at a clubhouse, in person, the way everything else here does. That also makes running from one city toward another literal: you would be running between the places the money is.

Box Fraise would take a cut. Which is a description of Visa, so it is worth saying exactly where the difference is rather than claiming to be nicer about the same arrangement.

  • 01No credit. Money has to be brought in before it can be spent, so nobody can be lent anything. That is not restraint, it is the shape of the thing — a cash-in system cannot extend credit. Credit is where the real extraction lives.
  • 02No record to sell. A card network's cut is not the business; what you bought is the business. If the ledger is never advertising inventory, the cut is only a price.
  • 03One veto, published. Card networks hold an unpublished and changing opinion about what you are allowed to sell. Box Fraise would hold exactly one condition — accept the month — and it is written on a page you can read before you join.

It does not exist yet. Nothing about it has been decided — not the rate, not the name, not whether it converts back — and none of that is worth settling before there is more than one clubhouse to exchange it at.

The nearest thing that is already true. Every dollar this project has taken so far has been cash, in person, at a run: the sticker and the donation, and the five dollars to enter the race. The argument came after the habit.

And tipping. Tipping did not inflate because people became more generous. It inflated when a terminal started asking, with a default, while somebody watched. That is coercion by interface, and it is the same complaint as the advertising one. The businesses that refuse it.
