Why it is asked

The oldest version of this. When the plebeians of Rome had no other leverage, they left. Not a refusal to work — a departure, out of the city, together. Rome had to look at the space where they had been. They did it more than once, across centuries, and it stopped being a protest and became part of how the city worked. It has a name: secessio plebis.

The modern one. Once a year, for a month, the platform leaves. A run out of one city toward another, and everybody who wants to come, comes.

It is a strike, not a holiday. A holiday is a trick that works once. This is a scheduled reminder that a city runs on people who are able to leave it, and it is there to keep the rest of society in check.

Businesses agree to it before they join. It is a feature of the platform, not something done to a business that never saw it coming. Accepting the month is a condition of being on Box Fraise — which means every business on it knows exactly which month it loses, and can plan a year ahead. Businesses that are not on the platform lose people too. They just do not know who, or when.

Anyone can run. The count is the platform's. Nobody is asked what they do for a living at the start of a run. But the people who make it a strike rather than a crowd are the ones working at businesses that agreed to it, and Box Fraise knows who they are. A withdrawal nobody can total is only absenteeism.

It does not exist yet. Running away from somewhere means running toward somewhere, so it needs clubhouses in more than one city. That is the next thing to build.

The question is already open. It is one of the three things a member can answer on gurgle, and you get an account by turning up to the run club.
